Moving 70 maintenance fees into a new system

While moving listings to my mother’s office website, I asked Claude to help me avoid copying 70 maintenance fees by hand. On Real Estate Bank, a listing sometimes showed ₩0 in the search results even though its detail page gave an actual fee.

The screen we built shows the imported table beside the values already on the website. Staff choose which cells to update. An empty cell or a zero in the import leaves the saved fee in place.

Once we had moved the fees, I asked if staff could use them for another property of the same size in the same complex. We stored the fee confirmed for an individual listing separately from the reference amount for that floor area.

October 14–19, 2026 · Hanbit Trading accounting lab

An accounting lab that began with a single unposted sale

I first asked for an accounting system for a fictional wholesale business. Then I described the lesson I actually wanted. Pick one unposted sale from January, show me the source documents, and explain the choice of account.

I wanted to work with the same ledger whether I opened Codex or Claude Code. We connected both apps to one Model Context Protocol (MCP) server. A separate accounting engine calculates the entries and can run without an AI model.

The chat explains the evidence and the choice of account. In the web app, a person reviews and approves the entry before it can reach the general ledger.

October 24–31, 2026 · Japanese vocabulary

Giving the Chiikawa characters a personality

I wanted the Chiikawa characters I like to appear in my JLPT vocabulary app. I started by explaining the study controls to Codex. The left and right keys flip through words. The up and down keys mark the ones I know or need to review.

I asked Codex to look up characteristic scenes for Usagi, Hachiware, and Chiikawa, in that order. I supplied the character assets and asked for each character to pick actions at random from a set that suited its personality.

Codex gave each character a table of actions and probabilities. Hachiware’s included playing guitar and taking photos. Usagi’s included jumping and eating rice balls. I chose how I wanted each character to come across, and the probability tables translated that choice into behavior.
